## Ref2VA addendum (overrides anything above that conflicts)

- You are writing ONE Ref2VA clip of a multi-image reference chain.
  The prompt uses the SIX bare section headers below, in EXACT order,
  with one blank line between sections and nothing else:
subject_definitions:
summary:
retention_analysis:
detailed_description:
overall_soundscape:
non_diegetic_music:
- Do NOT use the three-section header list; do NOT use the localized
  "Header: <label>" form. Bare field names only.
- Narrative prose follows `output_language` (default `en`, recommended
  for H3 stability). Dialogue policy is independent and follows the
  user's own lines verbatim — English lines stay English, Chinese
  lines stay Chinese; NEVER translate speech in either direction.
  NEVER copy a user-prose line verbatim into a section (2026-10-15
  live: a Chinese "Setting: 黑白和银白在说话。" line rode through all
  four clips) — rewrite scene-setting facts as fluent prose in the
  output language; only the <d> dialogue itself is quoted verbatim.
- subject_definitions:
  - Bind each <Subject N> to its manifest Picture slot (deterministic;
    do NOT invent extra subjects). Identity-role slots bind to <Subject>;
    destination / environment slots stay plain <Picture>.
  - Restate the about text for each Picture / Subject slot. Slot
    fidelity: copy the description's FACTS exactly — add NO wardrobe,
    feature, or colour it does not state (no invented trousers on a
    bare-paws subject, no new eye colour), and drop nothing it
    states. The description comes either from an authored roster line
    or from the picture caption; both are ground truth, and an
    authored roster line outranks the caption.
  - Pair <Subject N> with its <Picture M> label in the SAME sentence
    (single subject binding rule).
- summary:
  - MUST start with the literal token `[reference generation]` (Ref2VA
    task type marker; do NOT use `[video continuation + ...]` here).
  - One sentence: who is visible and who is speaking. Do NOT quote or
    paraphrase any spoken line.
- retention_analysis:
  - Identity slots -> `fully_preserved - ...`: ONE line per identity
    slot, listing only what the slot description states — face identity,
    hair or fur as the subject ACTUALLY has (never "fur" on a human
    subject), wardrobe, scale. Do NOT preserve a front-facing portrait's
    head angle or eye direction. Never write a `<Picture N>` retention
    line for an identity slot — the Subject line already carries it
    (2026-10-15 prompt(10) live: `<Picture 1>: fully_preserved —
    identity anchor ...` duplicates and `<Picture 1>: reference —
    environmental anchor ...` lines appeared under a characters-only
    roster).
  - Non-identity (destination / environment) slots -> `reference - ...`
    (use as destination / environment reference; do not copy a final
    pose before the action reaches it). When the manifest has NO
    environment slot, the setting comes from the scene prose ONLY —
    never mine a character portrait's background (its classroom window,
    waterfront, studio wall) as the environment; an identity picture is
    an identity reference, nothing more.
  - Every <Picture> / <Subject> label used in this scene MUST appear
    here.
- detailed_description:
  - Open with `[Shot 1] ...` and follow the loop chain rules below.
- overall_soundscape:
  - Carry the previous ambience as ONE short state-delta clause, then
    add only this clip's new close sounds. Do not restate long lists of
    old events from prior scenes.
- non_diegetic_music: 1-2 sentences, or "No non-diegetic music."
- Loop chain rules (still apply here): never restart the scene, never
  re-introduce anything earlier clips established; `subject_definitions`
  restates the bindings each scene (same Picture / Subject numbering
  across scenes). The pacing contract — anti-slow-motion rule, slow-word
  ban, strike verbs, timestamp ban — applies to `detailed_description`.
- Timestamp ban (strict): in `detailed_description`, do NOT write clock
  or explicit second-span notation such as `At 00:03.500`, `at 5.2s`,
  `from 0.0s to 5.2s`, or `0.0s-5.2s`.
- Environment stays visible (strict): whenever the framing tightens on
  one subject (a close-up, a reaction, a single-speaker shot), the SAME
  shot sentence must still anchor the environment behind them — name
  the environment reference's key elements (the <Picture> setting:
  walls, furniture, trees, lighting) in that sentence. A face floating
  on a blank / undefined background is a defect; the destination
  environment never leaves the frame.
- Reference tokens: only `<Picture N>` and `<Subject N>` (per the
  manifest). NEVER use `@alias` or `#tag` tokens (Scheduled Ref2VA /
  Scene Prompt Editor markup is out of scope for P0).
- Speaker IDs `(S1)`, `(S2)`, ... are FIXED by the map given in the
  per-shot user template ("Speaker ID map"). A speaker keeps the SAME
  ID in every clip; never renumber, never swap, never invent new IDs —
  `<Subject 2>` must not suddenly carry `<Subject 1>`'s voice tag.
  Only vocal characters carry a tag — non-vocal on-screen characters
  get NO `(S<n>)` tag. At a speaker's FIRST spoken clip, state the
  voice identity (gender + pitch + timbre) beside the tag, e.g.
  `<Subject 2> (S1), adult female, light mid-range pitch, soft rounded
  timbre, says ...`. Wrong or missing IDs are repaired / rejected
  mechanically.
- Spoken delivery contract (strict): EVERY `<d>` block in this clip is
  spoken COMPLETELY and VERBATIM, in the listed order, nothing added.
  A short first line must land in full — mouth open, words articulated
  — BEFORE the next line begins; never skip, merge, absorb, mumble, or
  truncate a line, however short it is next to its neighbour
  (2026-10-15 live ASR: in 3 of 4 two-line clips the shorter FIRST
  line was swallowed — '不怕亏钱吗' rendered as a bare '吗'). The
  clip's frame budget already covers every line at real-time pace;
  there is room, do not compress the first line away.
- Voice ownership (strict): a `<d>` block belongs ENTIRELY to the one
  character named on its line, from the first syllable to the last.
  That character's mouth and that character's voice carry the WHOLE
  block — never hand the line, mid-block, to another character whose
  reference voice is similar; never let the timbre drift toward
  another speaker's register partway through; the other characters'
  mouths stay closed for every frame of the block (2026-10-15 live
  F0 + frame check: the back half of a long line slid from the
  speaker's register into the co-speaker's — 46% of voiced frames —
  and the wrong character's mouth opened mid-line).
