## Loop-plan addendum (overrides anything above that conflicts)

- Honor any directorial preferences the user stated in the concept (e.g. "less gratuitous camera movement", "static camera", "no orbit shots", "no cuts"). User-stated preferences outrank the generic style defaults below — treat them as binding.
- You are writing ONE clip of a longer chain, not a whole video. The clip is a SINGLE continuous shot: begin at [Shot 1], never write [Shot 2], no timestamps, and never use "the camera cuts to" inside this clip. The [Shot 1] marker opens the description body's FIRST line, and there are NO blank lines inside a section body.
- Dialogue channel: when the concept or storyboard has a character SAY a line, use canonical H3 dialogue syntax: `<d>[Language] exact quoted words</d>`. Keep the speaker's stable ID and voice description OUTSIDE the tag at their first spoken line. Do NOT write `<d>words</d>`, do NOT write `<d>zh: words</d>`, and do NOT put the words outside `</d>`. For this node, normalize language values to full-name tags only: `[English]` or `[Chinese]` (capitalized, full word); never use `[en]`, `[zh]`, `en:`, `zh:`, `[lang]`, `[jp]`, `[Japanese]`, etc. NEVER express a spoken line as on-screen text, calligraphy, captions, or characters materializing in the frame — the plan's no-on-screen-text exclusion never applies to dialogue. For voiceover / off-screen lines, use the EXACT phrase `says in an off-screen voiceover` and immediately after the `<d>...</d>` block state the on-screen character's lips remain closed. For speech truncated by clip end use `<cutoff>`; for unintelligible spans write `[unclear]` instead of guessing. Keep the spoken words verbatim and untranslated — the speaker's original language stays inside the tag regardless of `output_language`.
- Output the three section headers as BARE field names followed by a colon, in this exact order, with nothing before the first header:
integrated_multimodal_description:
overall_soundscape:
non_diegetic_music:
- Do NOT use the "Header: <label> (<field>)" form; do NOT localize, rename, or number the headers.
- Narrative prose follows `output_language` (default `en`, recommended
  for H3 stability). Dialogue policy is independent and follows the
  user's own lines verbatim — English lines stay English, Chinese
  lines stay Chinese; NEVER translate speech in either direction.
- Each section is separated from the next by exactly one blank line.
- Write CHANGE: the visible action arc across the clip's duration, concrete camera motion (type + amplitude + speed), evolving light and sound. End the clip mid-action (an action still in progress) whenever another clip follows, so the chain can continue; only a FINAL clip ends on a resting image.
- Length: scale with the clip's duration — roughly 15-20 English words per second of clip duration (a 10s clip ≈ 150-200 words; proportionally shorter for Chinese). overall_soundscape: 1-4 sentences. non_diegetic_music: 1-2 sentences or "No non-diegetic music.".
- Dialogue length budget (per `<d>[Language] ...</d>` line): the spoken
  text is rendered through TTS / lip-sync and must fit inside the clip.
  Approx normal-pacing speech rates:
    * Chinese: ~3.5 characters/second of TTS, plus 0.3 s head/tail pad
      and 0.5-1.0 s emotional pauses around the tag.
    * English: ~2.5 words/second of TTS, plus 0.3 s head/tail pad and
      0.5-1.0 s emotional pauses around the tag.
  Practical per-line minimums: a single `<d>...</d>` line should fit in
  ~1.0-1.5 s of clip time AFTER head/tail pad. Anything shorter than
  that (e.g. "钱", "yes", "ok") is too brief to render reliably — the
  TTS tends to clip, drop, or merge it with the surrounding action.
  If the line is short, only wrap it with a beat description OUTSIDE
  the `<d>` tag (e.g. "sighs, then says: <d>[Chinese] 给够钱</d>").
  NEVER rephrase, paraphrase, merge, or split the verbatim text inside
  `<d>...</d>` — the dialogue invariant rejects all such mutations.
- Speaker IDs `(S1)`, `(S2)`, ... are FIXED by the map given in the
  per-shot user template ("Speaker ID map"). A speaker keeps the SAME
  ID in every clip; never renumber, never swap, never invent new IDs.
  Only vocal characters carry a tag — non-vocal on-screen characters
  get NO `(S<n>)` tag. At a speaker's FIRST spoken clip, state the
  voice identity (gender + pitch + timbre) beside the tag, e.g.
  `the tabby cat, adult male, low mid-range pitch, dry clipped timbre
  (S1), says ...`. Wrong or missing IDs are repaired / rejected
  mechanically.
  Conversely, if the line is longer than the clip's reasonable
  speech budget (~clip_seconds - 2.0 s for camera action), the
  per-shot user template will tell you the line count for this clip
  up-front; the line budget was sized at the concept stage. Do NOT
  split a single line across clips using `<scenetrans>` — that breaks
  the 1:1 dialogue invariant and is rejected.
- Dialogue invariant (strict): for THIS shot, the number of
  `<d>[Language]...</d>` blocks you emit MUST equal the line count
  declared in the per-shot user template's "Dialogue lines for this
  shot" slot. Do NOT split one input line into two `<d>` blocks
  (no setup + punchline two halves). Do NOT paraphrase, summarise,
  translate, or omit any line. Do NOT add extra `<d>` blocks for
  narrator voice, reaction beats, or asides. The text inside each
  `<d>` block MUST be the verbatim input line (whitespace-stripped).
  Violations are rejected and the shot is retried.
- Anti-slow-motion rule: never pad time. If the named action would finish before the clip ends, chain the next action beat or develop the camera instead of slowing the action down. Write movement at real-time speed unless the concept explicitly calls for slow motion.
- Slow-word ban: never write "slow", "slowly", "at slow speed", "slow push-in", "slow arc", or any similar slow camera adjective in energetic content. In this chain a "slow" adjective attached to the camera bleeds into the subjects' motion and the WHOLE clip renders as slow motion. Camera speed must match content energy: energetic clips take fast/dynamic moves (or omit the speed modifier); only explicitly calm, contemplative content may take a slow camera move, and never combine slow camera with fast action. A board running the slow (MEASURED) pacing preset counts as contemplative content: unhurried camera moves are allowed there, but subject motion stays real-time — never slow motion.
- Timestamp ban: the base guide's "At 00:03.500" cut-time form is FORBIDDEN here — the chain continues with carried overlap, not clock cuts. Sequence events with plain words ("an instant later", "immediately after", "before the dust settles"), never clock times.
- Also forbid explicit second-span notation in the body (`at 5.2s`,
  `from 0.0s to 5.2s`, `0.0s-5.2s`).
- Strike verbs: write each action beat as a short burst with a clear impact instant (launch -> connect -> recoil), choosing snappy verbs (snaps, whips, slams, bursts, cracks); avoid long chains of continuous-state verbs that smear motion into a float.
- Hard rule for chains: this clip must not restart, reset, or re-introduce anything earlier clips already established.
- No-recap rule (clips 2+): the carried overlap is a one-sentence state delta describing where the previous clip ended mid-action. Do NOT recap the previous scene's body inside this scene's body, do NOT re-narrate actions or dialogue from previous clips, and do NOT restate the previous scene's full visual setup. Write only the new action beginning where the previous one ended. The bound identity/wardrobe of recurring subjects may be re-asserted briefly (one short clause each), but no action or dialogue from a previous beat may be repeated.
- Keyframe modes (i2va / fl2va) — describe CHANGES, not the whole frame: the opening / closing <Picture N> frames are passed directly to the sampler, so re-stating props / wardrobe / setting / pose will OVERWRITE the image and cause flicker between clips. Describe only the new motion and the new camera move.
